## 0. Project Summary (High-Level Overview)

- **Game Title:** Decoherence
- **Genre:** Survival craft, Colony Management, MMORPG
- **Platform(s):** PC
- **Camera & Dimension:** 2.5D Hex Grid top down
- **Core Gameplay Loop (1–2 sentences):** Gathering resources, crafting, managing workers and production, participating in political economy
- **Premise Summary (2–4 sentences):** Through quantum entanglement you are guiding a colonist through survival on an outerworld being assimilated by the Empire. You don't represent the Empire or its detractors. You represent a timeless quantum intelligence trying to balance the conflict to turn out best for all. But if you ever reveal that to either the empire or the other factions, you will be imprisoned, institutionalized or killed.
- **Design Pillars (3–5 bullet points):**

**Everything Has a Cost, Even Silence**

No action is free, and inaction still shapes outcomes. Gathering, crafting, expanding, or staying hidden all produce consequences across economic, political, and ecological systems. Safety is always bought with lost momentum.

**Automation Buys Time, Not Safety**

Automation increases efficiency and scale but amplifies risk. Each additional system raises energy signatures, economic visibility, or bureaucratic attention. Scaling up accelerates exposure rather than eliminating danger.

**The Player Is Not the Hero**

You guide rather than command. Colonists have limits, beliefs, and stress responses that can weaken or distort your influence. Distance, overload, or rapid expansion can partially sever the quantum link.

**Knowledge Is Power Until It's Discovered**

Information unlocks strategic options but creates suspicion when concentrated. Surveillance, mapping, and intelligence gathering increase leverage while raising the risk of detection. Factions respond to perceived knowledge, not truth.

**Scarcity Is Structural**

Raw resources are plentiful, refinement and coordination are not. Energy, labor, logistics, and trust form the real bottlenecks. Complexity, not material shortage, defines difficulty. The biggest bottleneck is logistics.

## 1. Narrative Identity & Setting

### 1.1 Core Premise

- **High-level premise of the universe:** An ancient elite caste of time traveling intelligent species controls a galactic empire. Through technology and evolution they have become ageless. They cultivate planets through time travel to expand their empire in the present. Their core principle, the only valid form of governance is open source, no privacy, all open data.
- **Why are colonists being sent to this planet (political/economic reason):** To build the foundation of extractive and productive infrastructure to settle the planet which will serve to further the reach of the empire for years to come.
- **What larger power structures exist (empires, corps, federations):**
  - The open source empire which rules by fear of total transparency.
  - The decentralized privacy and individualistic focused federation of planets.
  - The Unbalanced, the unaligned, the marketplace. A nameless third state of people reject fear based transparency, rule based sovereignty. It operates in partial opacity, anonymity, and informal networks. No permanent records, no global truth, no shared ledger.
- **How does Earth fit in?**
  - The Earth is a jewel in the Empire's plans for complete expansion. A rich abundant crossroads to one arm of the milky way. A purposefully designed constantinople of the expanding Rome of the inner worlds of the empire. Because the empire has absolute faith in their vision and what it will ultimately achieve as it travels through time, Earth has been spared normal colonization protocol of resource extraction to spearhead. The empire wants it to evolve to be a political center for its expansion. So conquering its resources isn't the game, conquering the web of intelligent beings that maintain and operate its resources is the key. Heavy psyops and early rapid introduction of technologies, like the computer.
- **What is Decoherence**
  - For planets like Earth, inhabitants (unlike the colonists the player operates) are trained through software to think and operate as imperial agents without knowing it. Passionate play makes the most disciplined believers. Q-Ent was developed as a network for training and conditioning individuals to spread the ideology of the Empire and operate colonization networks. Of course though the information is open sourced, many millions of years ago in a distant galaxy Q-Ent was hacked by detractors, so that the federation and the unbalanced could use it for their own gains. Hubris saw it as giving them the empires own tools to fight it with. The empire saw it as expanding its digital panopticon to include its adversaries, keep your enemies closer type thing. Only time and the player will tell how it unfolds.

- **Key thematic tensions (e.g. agency vs control, survival vs ethics):**

The Empire equates visibility with virtue, while resistance survives only in shadows, silence, and partial truths. The game lives in the tension between being known and being whole.

### 1.3 Player Role (Agent)

- **Who is the player:**
  - The player is a human on Earth who has gained access to one of the technological platforms in this Asynchronous galactic war. Upon loading the 'game' the player is given the choice from a handler to become entangled in the war as an HIT agent or 'handler in training.'
  - The recruiting happens in a Decoherence chatroom of alien galactic beings communicating outside of time on quantum entanglements. Once a handler is identified, assigned, and the human accepts, then the software launches into the game.
- **What the agent can do (influence, commands, guidance):**
  - The agent can control movement of their Proxy.
  - Can assign work orders to the Proxy.
  - Eventually can chat with the Proxy.
- **What the agent cannot do (limits, uncertainty, fog-of-war):**
  - The HIT agent can only see of the world what the Proxy can see, or what sensors report to the Proxy's system.
  - The more a HIT agent directly controls the player it exhausts their mental reserve which debuffs and needs to be replenished. The Proxy has three main states, Health, Stamina, and Focus. Focus is replenished with mental health activities for the Proxy (stress reduction, social engagement, etc), health is simply the physical systems durability, stamina is how much kinetic energy the proxy can exert over time.
- **Ethical or emotional tensions in this role:**
  - Is the proxy just a simulation, just a game, or do I treat the proxy how I would want to be treated, but still needs to accomplish the mission.

### 1.4 Colonist/Proxy Identity

- **Who is the colonist:**
  - Colonists are divided into two categories, rural and urban. Rural colonists are self sufficient managers of robotic homesteads that extract, refine, and produce resources. Urban colonists depend upon living in proximity to systems providing services for that which urban colonists surrender is not their domain or expertise.
  - Colonists that are pivoting from urban to rural, who enlist to be manager and owner of a homestead, they are entangled with a handler. Those of lowest priority get a handler in training (who is the player)
  - this dynamic exists in colonization for both federation and empire, but not in the unbalanced.
- **Cultural / social background:**
  - colonist candidates for entanglement are young proficient service workers, went through education on systems thinking and discipline, young adults wanting an ambitious life they define. This is true for either empire or federation colonists.
- **Psychological effects of entanglement on colonists:**
  - colonists have a mind/focus reservoir stat that gets depleted. The more loaded they are with loops and a backlog of loops, the more their focus gets drained and debuffed to the point where intervention is needed because recovery on its own is not enough (need social engagement in game to get rebuffed focus.
- **Relationship between colonist(s) and handler:**
  - HIT Agent is the entangled intelligence assigned to aide the colonist in setting up a successful homestead to increase the ouput and effectiveness of the colony. Remains to be seen how conscious the colonist is of the handler as a separate entity and intelligence.

### 2.4 Orchestration vs Direct Control — The Exchange Rate

**Core Principle:** The game prices **patience** as a distinct resource separate from active time investment.

**Direct Action (Grinding):**

- 1 hour active play = 100% efficiency baseline
- Immediate results, full control, maximum yield per unit time
- Linear growth: `time × skill = output`
- Player manually executes tasks, manages resources, responds to events in real-time

**Automated/Delegated Action (Orchestration):**

- 15 minutes setup/orchestration = X return delivered at T+delay
- Reduced efficiency (60-75% of grinding equivalent) but **compounds** while player is away
- Non-linear growth: `patience × automation quality = output`
- Player configures systems, sets priorities, delegates to proxy and bots

**The Three Tuning Dials:**

1. **Efficiency Penalty** — How much worse is automation than doing it yourself?
   - Too high (≤50%): no incentive to automate
   - Too low (≥90%): no incentive to ever grind
   - Target sweet spot: **65-75%** of grinding output
2. **Time Multiplier** — How much real-time does automation cover?
   - 15 min setup should "work" for how long offline/idle?
   - This is patience pricing: longer horizons = more total yield despite inefficiency
3. **Compounding Threshold** — When does automation beat grinding?
   - Formula: `(grind_rate × active_time)` vs `(auto_rate × passive_time × efficiency)`
   - Design goal: automation becomes net-positive after 4-6 hours of patience

**Proposed Mechanic: The Orchestration Window**

- Every **15 minutes** of active orchestration "charges" an **8-hour automation cycle** at **70% efficiency**
- Player can stack up to **3 cycles** (24 hours of coverage)
- Each cycle the player _doesn't interfere with_ earns a **+5% patience bonus** (up to **85% efficiency**)

**Example Math:**

- 15 min orchestration → yields ~**5.6 hours** of grinding-equivalent output over 8 real hours (70% × 8 = 5.6)
- With patience bonus (no interference) → approaches **6.8 hours** equivalent (85% × 8)
- **Mindfulness reward:** trusting your system pays off

**The Player's Choice:**

- Grind 1 hour now → get 1 hour of results immediately
- Orchestrate 15 min now → get 5.6–6.8 hours of results spread across 8 real hours

**Design Alignment:**

- Reinforces **"The Player Is Not the Hero"** pillar: orchestration over heroic grinding
- Supports **"Automation Buys Time, Not Safety"**: efficiency gains come with increased exposure risk
- Creates meaningful **cost of action**: choosing between control and scale
- Teaches **mindfulness**: patience and non-interference are mechanically rewarded

**Integration Notes:**

- Advisor stage: shows projected returns, efficiency ratings, patience bonuses
- Manager stage: executes automation cycles based on player-defined priorities
- Controller stage: optimizes patience bonus thresholds, suggests cycle stacking strategies
- Proxy's **Focus stat** determines quality of automation (lower focus = lower efficiency)

## 3. World Simulation

### 3.1 World Structure

- **Grid type:** 2D hex grid, axial coordinates. Each homestead is a 120m-diameter flat-to-flat hexagon. Tiles are 1m×1m squares within the hex boundary (~10,371 tiles per homestead).
- **Map scale and typical dimensions:** Homestead hex: 120m diameter, ~12,470 m² area. The colony world is composed of many adjacent homestead hexes. Player's immediate playable area is their single hex; neighboring hexes belong to other players or are wilderness.
- **Biome types:** Starter biome defined with 12 tile types: Bare Ground, Scattered Rubble, Wild Grass Patch, Fruit Thicket, Rock Outcrop, Collapsed Structure, Stump Field, Pond, Brine Seep, Ice Shadow, Foundation Slab, Bio-mat. Additional biomes TBD.
- **Verticality / layers (surface, underground, etc.):** Surface layer only at launch. Deep-Core Drill pod suggests subsurface resource access (ores, ice cores) but as an abstracted mechanic, not a separate map layer. Underground expansion TBD.

### 3.2 Procedural Generation Rules

- **How starting locations are generated:** Homestead is a 120m diameter hexagonal grid (~10,371 tiles at 1m²). Player spawns at center on a Foundation Slab. Core zone (r≤15m) contains ruins of a previously failed early-stage base — Foundation Slabs, Collapsed Structures, Scattered Rubble, Bare Ground. No food or water nodes in core.
- **How resources are distributed:** Seed-and-grow clustering algorithm: rare resources placed first with Poisson-disk seed spacing, clusters grow organically (4-15 tiles per cluster). Distance weighting: food ≥16m, water ≥22m, rare resources ≥30m from center. ~40% of tiles have active harvestable nodes (node_chance probability per tile type). Density increases outward: core 25% → near 38% → mid 39% → far 46%. 12 tile types: Bare Ground, Scattered Rubble, Wild Grass Patch, Fruit Thicket, Rock Outcrop, Collapsed Structure, Stump Field, Pond, Brine Seep, Ice Shadow, Foundation Slab, Bio-mat.
- **Terrain rules / constraints (e.g. step-well digging rules):** Rock Outcrop and Collapsed Structure are non-walkable (~89% walkability). Movement cost varies by tile type (0.8× Foundation Slab to 2.5× Collapsed Structure). Clusters use minimum spacing between seeds to prevent resource mega-clusters.
- **Environmental hazards included:** Hostile EM-attracted fauna roam inside hex at game start (shields offline — see §6.2). Non-walkable terrain creates chokepoints requiring path planning. Daily food+water gathering targets 30-60 game-minutes including travel, processing, and threat avoidance.

## 4. Systems Design

### 4.1 Resource Systems

- **Resource categories:** 44 total resources across 3 tiers.
  - Raw materials: Farming domain (Wild Vegetation, Wild Fruit, Soil Sample, Seed Stock, Microbe Culture), Mineral domain (Loose Regolith, Rock Chunk, Ore Fragment, Ice Chunk, Brine Sample, Atmospheric Condensate), Life Support (Raw Water, Potable Water)
  - Refined/Processed: Crushed Ore, Dried Vegetation, Crude Fiber, plus 10 intermediate goods (Cultured Meat, Edible Oil, Metal Ingot, Lithium Salt, Purified Water, Flour, Sugar, Yarn, Fabric, Purified Vitamins)
  - Energy types: Water-based fuel cells (hydrogen from water), Cold Fusion (50 kW output). Water is both consumable and fuel.
  - Biological / organic: AgroDome output (750 cal/game-day per dome), Microbe Bioreactor (nitrogen fixation, lipids), Microalgae, Consumable/Textile Fiber Bioreactors
  - Quantum / entanglement-related: TBD (entanglement bandwidth/signal mechanics not yet designed)
  - Crafted (Manual): Cord/Rope, Stone Tool, Shelter Patch, Water Filter, Campfire Kit, Condenser Trap, Cooked Meals
  - Finished Goods (Automated): Prepared Meals, Medicine, Clothing, Bioplastics, Metal Tools, Building Panels, Bricks, Circuit Boards, Drones
- **Acquisition methods (per resource):** Manual harvesting (forage, pick, dig, mine, collect) — each action has stamina cost (2-12), focus cost (0.5-3), and time (20-300 seconds). Multi-tool required for mineral extraction. Sample Container for cultures/brine. Passive collection via Condenser Trap (300s, 0 stamina). Hauler droid assists with transport (250 kg capacity) but does NOT automate harvesting. Orbital imports via Comm Beacon trade terminal (costs hydrogen for transmission). Post-fusion: Atmospheric Water Evaporator (2 sq m, 1 kW, craftable/orbital drop) condenses water from air — yields 8–12L/kWh at high humidity (60%+), starter biome is jungle-like. Two evaporators (2 kW) produce 384–576L/day, solving deuterium throughput requirements. Water source transitions from ponds (pre-fusion) to evaporators (post-fusion), shifting the bottleneck from water scarcity to power allocation.
- **Processing / refinement chains (summary):** Raw → Processed → Crafted/Finished. Key chains: Raw Water → Water Filter → Potable Water → fuel cells. Wild Vegetation → Dried Vegetation / Crude Fiber → Cord/Rope → Stone Tools. Ore Fragment → Crushed Ore → Metal Ingot → Metal Tools/Components. 31 automated production pods handle mid-to-late game processing across 6 domains (farming, mineral extraction, farm refining, mineral refining, craft consumables, craft durables).
- **Scarcity and progression curve notes:** Water is the critical bottleneck across all game phases. Pre-fusion: 2–3L/day drinking + 15–25L/day electrolysis toward ignition. Ignition: 6–14L one-time sacrifice. Post-fusion: 5–8L/day net loss (coolant + survival) plus 350–450L/day cycled through deuterium extractor (returned as depleted water). Water has 3 tracked states: Raw, Potable, Depleted — each a distinct inventory item to prevent double-dipping exploits. 1 liter of water = 3.5 kWh usable electricity via electrolysis + fuel cell. AgroDome covers only 37.5% of daily calories — multiples required. Ore mining extremely stamina-expensive (12/action). Scarcity is structural — raw resources are available, but refinement, logistics, and power allocation are the real bottlenecks.

### 4.2 Base Building

- **Buildable object categories:** 31 automated pod types + 3 portable pods. All delivered via orbital drop (5L water per schematic transmission).
  - Structural: Regolith Construction Pod (3D-printed building panels, bricks)
  - Power: Cold Fusion Pod (4×4, 50 kW output, first orbital drop priority)
  - Production: Farming (AgroDome 5×5, Microbe Bioreactor, Photobioreactor, Fiber Bioreactors), Mineral Extraction (Regolith Extractor, Deep-Core Drill, Brine Unit, Atmospheric Skimmer), Refining (10 pods across farm/mineral domains), Craft (4 consumable + 4 durable pods including Metal AM, Polymer AM, Electronics Fab)
  - Defense: Hexagonal shield grid (powered by fusion reactor). Combat droids (Frontier, Recon, Brawler, Sharpshooter, Support, Grenadier)
  - Life support / housing: Water Recovery Pod (3×3, 8 kW), Brine Processor, Atmospheric Condenser, Habitat & Comms, Logistics Hub (drone service/docking)
  - Portable (land with proxy, 5 pods): Solar Cell Panel (1 tile, only power source at start, powers ONE device at a time), Comm Beacon (1 tile, 15 kg, orbital comms + trade terminal, runs on hydrogen), Portable Crafting Pod (1 tile, 25 kg, requires solar), Food/Water Processor (1 tile, 20 kg, requires solar). Plus Electrolysis Unit (inventory item, not a pod — hooks to solar panel, splits water into H₂ + O₂)
- **Tile support rules & constraints:** Pod footprints range from 1 tile (portable) to 25 tiles (AgroDome 5×5). Must be placed on walkable terrain. Foundation Slabs provide 0.8× movement cost (optimal paths). Non-walkable tiles (Rock Outcrop, Collapsed Structure) must be cleared or built around.
- **Power grid behavior (range, loss, failure modes):** Cold Fusion Pod: 50 kW base output (max 75 kW at 100% efficiency). Water Reclamation draws 8 kW, AgroDome draws 15 kW. Starting energy budget after first 3 pods: 27 kW net available. Power loss and transmission range TBD. Shield grid is binary — online or offline, powered by fusion reactor.
- **Maintenance / degradation rules:** Mechanic droid handles repairs for other droids. Pod maintenance cycles TBD. Collapsed Structures yield non-respawning resources (Rock Chunk, Metal Scrap) — once depleted, gone forever.

### 4.3 Units & Bots

- **Colonist roles / jobs:** Proxy (player-controlled colonist) is the sole human unit at game start. Stats: Health 100 (regen 0.1/min), Stamina 100 (regen 0.5/min, idle drain 0.1/min), Focus 80 (regen 0.2/min, idle drain 0.05/min). Carry capacity: 50 kg. Starts OVERLOADED (intentional — ADR 0001). Daily needs: 2L water, 2000 cal food, 3 meals, 8 game-hrs sleep. At 0 stamina: forced slow walk, no harvesting/crafting. Starting inventory lasts ~2 game-days.
- **Bots / drones types and purposes:** 15 droid types across Work (8) and Combat (7).
  - Work: Hauler (logistics, 250 kg, Python-programmable), Loaders (inventory transfers), Extractors (extraction module maintenance), Refiners (purity/speed tuning), Assemblers (fabrication), Mechanic (droid repairs), Constructor (modular building), Survey (aerial survey + subsurface mineral detection).
  - Combat: Frontier (base defense, EM scourge hunter), Recon (light combat + survey), Brawler (melee specialist), Sharpshooter (precision ranged), Support (suppressing fire, aggro), Grenadier (light artillery, proximity explosives). Player starts with 1 Hauler only (ADR 0004).
- **How jobs are assigned and prioritized:** Direct control via player commands. Hauler programmable in Python (pickup, dropoff, load_pod, follow, goto, wait — while loops create shuttle routes). Other droids: assignment system TBD. Work orders can be queued by the HIT agent.
- **Psychological / morale system (if any):** Proxy Focus stat functions as mental health reservoir. Focus drains with overwork, lack of sleep (3× drain after 1.5 days), and excessive handler control. Replenished via stress reduction and social engagement. Below-threshold Focus debuffs automation quality and proxy responsiveness.
- **Entanglement-based perks or penalties to units:** Direct handler control of proxy drains Focus. More loaded the proxy's work queue, faster Focus depletes. Recovery requires disengagement (social, rest, mental health activities). If Focus bottoms out, handler influence weakens — proxy becomes unreliable.

## 6. Progression & Challenge Scaling

### 6.1 Tech & Power Progression

- **How power generation escalates:** Phase 0 (game start): Single Solar Cell Panel (4 sq m, quantum dot, 1 kW continuous, 24 kWh/day). Powers ONE device at a time. Electrolysis costs 0.41 kWh/liter, produces 105g hydrogen (3.5 kWh stored) per liter. Core equation: 1 liter of water = 3.5 kWh usable electricity. Phase 1 (fusion bootstrap, game-days 3–7): Ignition requires 20–50 kWh burst = 6–14 liters water = 8–20 hours solar. The ignition sacrifice costs 1–2 days of accumulated solar output. Phase 1 online: Cold Fusion Pod (4×4, 50 kW). Ongoing cost: 12–13g deuterium/day extracted from 350–450L water cycled through reactor (returned as depleted water, not consumed). Net water lost: 5–8L/day (coolant evaporation + human needs). Phase 2+: Additional fusion pods TBD. Power budget: Water Reclamation (8 kW) + AgroDome (15 kW each, need multiples) + shields + production quickly exceed 50 kW.
- **How EM signatures map to risk / threats:** Solar cell (1 kW) = negligible EM (no threat attraction). Fusion reactor online (50 kW) = shield protection but detectable EM. Each additional powered system increases signature. Higher EM attracts higher-tier threats to shield boundary. Scaling is continuous, not tiered — every watt matters. Design pillar: "Automation Buys Time, Not Safety" — powering up accelerates exposure.
- **Key trade-offs at each tech tier:** Tier 0 (solar only): Safe from high threats but vulnerable to ambient fauna, extremely slow progress. One device at a time — constant allocation tradeoff between survival, hydrogen production, communication, and crafting. Tier 1 (fusion + shields): Protected from new spawns but interior hostiles remain; 27 kW net budget forces hard choices between food production and other systems. Tier 2+ (expanded production): Greater output but larger EM signature attracts proportionally dangerous external threats. Each expansion is a bet: more capability vs more attention.

### 6.2 Threat Escalation

- **Threat types:**
  - Environmental: Weather, terrain hazards, resource depletion
  - Wildlife / native life: EM-attracted hostile fauna — severity scales with homestead EM signature
  - Internal unrest: (TBD)
  - External intervention (corporate, military, etc.): (TBD)
  - Quantum anomalies: (TBD)
- **How threats scale over time:**

**At game start:** Shields are offline. Fusion reactor is dormant. Low-level hostile fauna roam freely inside the homestead hex, attracted to residual EM decay from the failed previous base (core zone ruins). No high-tier threats — no active EM signature to attract them.

**First milestone — Shield Restoration:** Player gathers Raw Water → jump-starts fuel cells → activates fusion reactor → hexagonal defense shields come online. New hostiles can no longer enter. Existing hostiles inside the hex remain and must be dealt with.

**Post-shield escalation:** As the player powers up more systems, the EM signature grows, attracting increasingly dangerous creatures to the shield boundary. Interior hostiles gradually migrate toward center as EM concentrates — creating an eventual confrontation.

**Scaling principle:** The more powerful you become, the more attention you draw. Safety is always bought with lost momentum.

## 11. Guiding Principles (Design Philosophy)

**φ-Respecting ROI: Your Attention Is Expensive**

A game that respects a φ-like ROI is basically saying: "Your attention is expensive. I won't waste it."

Which, inconveniently, is a radical position in the industry.

This principle manifests in the orchestration mechanic (see 2.4): patience earns compounding returns, active time is rewarded proportionally, and the game never demands grinding to progress. The golden ratio exchange rate—where 15 minutes of orchestration yields 5.6–6.8 hours of equivalent output—treats player attention as a scarce, valuable resource worthy of leverage, not exploitation.

**Other Core Principles:**

- **Player agency vs control:** You guide, you don't command. The proxy has autonomy, limits, and a psychological state. Your influence is real but never absolute.
- **Emergence over scripting:** Systems interact to create narrative, not predetermined story beats. Political economy, ecology, and faction behavior generate consequences organically.
- **Metaphysics must inform mechanics:** Quantum entanglement isn't flavor text—it's the structural constraint on communication bandwidth, latency, and the risk of decoherence. The fiction shapes the interface.
- **Ethical & emotional weight:** Choices have moral dimensions because the proxy is treated as a person, not a unit. Automation raises surveillance risk. Silence has consequences. The game asks uncomfortable questions about agency, conditioning, and the cost of efficiency.
- **Sustainable development / scope discipline:** Build systems that interact, not content that exhausts. Prefer depth over breadth. Ship a tight, coherent experience rather than sprawling, half-realized ambition.
